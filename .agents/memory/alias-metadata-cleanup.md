---
name: Alias and prototype metadata cleanup
description: Conservative matching and uncertainty rules for future Parent Decoder dictionary edits.
---

Short aliases should be published only when they are both distinctive and necessary. Ordinary words, initials, and ambiguous singular forms should be removed or replaced with a longer phrase; preserve the canonical record and add fictional positive/negative regression pairs for every removal.

**Why:** Exact token matching can turn everyday writing, names, labels, or initials into misleading decoder hits. Boundary warnings alone do not protect against a token that is itself too broad.

**How to apply:** Prefer phrase-level aliases and explicit innocent meanings. Keep intentional short abbreviations only when their context is distinctive, and exercise known nested overlaps with longer positive cases.

Prototype metadata should not assert unsupported popularity, currentness, lifecycle, or broad geographic distribution. Until a qualified review adds evidence, use uncertainty wording and keep prototype release status.

**Why:** The dictionary has no independent sources or verification dates, so claims such as “current,” “stable,” “widely recognized,” or “global” can overstate confidence even when the definition is otherwise useful.

**How to apply:** Normalize broad prototype metadata conservatively, preserve specific context only when it is clearly qualified, and never add invented sources, reviewers, dates, or cultural consensus.
